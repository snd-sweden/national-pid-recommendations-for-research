# Organisation and sustainability

_Last updated: 2026-09-28_

These recommendations concern the organisational side of PID work: policy and responsibilities, dependencies on external PID infrastructures, and follow-up.

## Adopt a PID policy with clear responsibilities

_Applies to: PID Managers and organisations selecting PID services._

!!! quote ""

    Document your organisation's PID practices in a PID policy, and assign named responsibility and lasting resources for PID work.

??? question "Why?"

    PIDs are a long-term commitment that outlives individual projects, systems and employees. A PID policy makes the organisation's commitments visible to users and partners, and makes sure that decisions on granularity, versions and withdrawn digital objects are made consistently. Without clear ownership, PID work tends to become fragmented, and responsibilities are easily lost when staff, systems or organisations change.

??? info "How?"

    - Publish the policy as a separate document or as part of a data policy or service documentation.
    - Cover at least:
        - which digital objects and phenomena are assigned PIDs, and with which PID systems;
        - rules for granularity, versions and identifier precedence (see [Reuse existing PIDs before minting new ones](referencing.md#reuse-existing-pids-before-minting-new-ones) and [Define rules for granularity and versions](assigning.md#define-rules-for-granularity-and-versions));
        - handling of withdrawn digital objects (see [Never delete or reassign a PID, and provide tombstone pages](assigning.md#never-delete-or-reassign-a-pid-and-provide-tombstone-pages));
        - who is responsible for registration, metadata quality, updates, incident handling and service continuity;
        - what happens to the organisation's PIDs if a service is closed, transferred or reorganised.
    - Resource PID work as a lasting task rather than a project.
    - Include PID-related skills in relevant roles and in training for research support staff.

See also: [PID ecosystem](../../pid-concepts/pid-ecosystem.md)

## Assess dependencies and plan for continuity

_Applies to: organisations selecting PID services._

!!! quote ""

    Assess the risks of depending on external PID infrastructures, and make sure your PIDs can survive changes both to the PID services you use and to your own organisation.

??? question "Why?"

    PIDs make the research information landscape depend on the PID infrastructures behind them. If a PID service is discontinued, changes its conditions or is acquired, the PIDs registered with it, and every reference to them, are at risk. Such risks can be organisational, economic, technical or legal. Services with open governance, transparent business models and published exit plans are easier to assess and less likely to fail without warning.

??? info "How?"

    - Include a risk assessment when adopting and reviewing PID services.
    - Prefer PID services operated by non-profit organisations with open, community-based governance, transparent business models and published exit or contingency plans.
    - Make sure that responsibility for your PIDs can be transferred to another PID Manager if your own service is closed or reorganised, and include this in agreements with providers and partners.
    - Participate in the governance of the PID infrastructures you depend on, directly or through national consortia; see [National coordination of PID usage](../national/index.md).

See also: [Persistence](../../pid-concepts/persistence.md)

## Monitor PID use and quality

_Applies to: PID Managers._

!!! quote ""

    Monitor the state of your PID work with a small set of indicators, and use the results to plan improvements.

??? question "Why?"

    Broken resolution, incomplete metadata and duplicate PIDs are rarely noticed by the organisation that caused them, but they affect everyone who relies on the PIDs. The same applies to content drift, where a PID still resolves but no longer leads to the content it originally identified. Indicators make problems visible early, show progress over time and help demonstrate the benefits of PID use within the organisation.

??? info "How?"

    - Track indicators such as:
        - resolution success of registered PIDs;
        - content drift, where a PID leads to something other than what it originally identified;
        - completeness of metadata, including PIDs for related entities;
        - duplicate PIDs;
        - the share of people, organisations, projects, grants and outputs in your systems that are identified and linked with PIDs.
    - For large collections, check resolution and content on a random sample of PIDs at regular intervals, with a sample size that gives the certainty you need.
    - Use the results to plan improvements and to show the benefits of PID use internally.
