# International actors

_Last updated: 2026-09-30_

## European Open Science Cloud

The original EOSC PID policy was published in 2020 by the EOSC FAIR and Architecture working groups[@rep-PersistentIdentifier-20]. It is not a national strategy, but sets expectations for how PIDs are used by service and infrastructure providers in EOSC. The policy is deliberately technology-neutral: it defines a PID as globally unique, persistent and resolvable, and sets requirements for PID service providers:

- PID services should be interoperable with each other and across research infrastructures.
- PID service providers should apply community governance embedded in global governance, provide their services at a justifiable cost, and have a public exit plan that ensures continuity of their PIDs if they cease to operate.
- PID providers and users are expected to commit to sustaining PID infrastructures in the long term.

The policy has since been revised by the EOSC Association's expert group on PIDs. Version 2.0, released in March 2026, is a community recommendation that has not yet been formally approved[@rep-CommunityRecommendation-26]. It keeps the same framework, but states its requirements more firmly and adds commitments to risk and lifecycle management, alignment with the Principles of Open Scholarly Infrastructure (POSI), and a coordination mechanism for EOSC PID service providers. The FAIR-IMPACT project has also drafted guidelines for PID managers, such as repositories, that write their own EOSC-compliant PID policy[@rep-D33Guidelines-24].

## Research Data Alliance

The Research Data Alliance (RDA) published a guide and checklist for national PID strategies in 2023, based on case studies from Australia, Canada, the Czech Republic, Finland, Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, South Korea and the United Kingdom[@rep-RDANational-23]. The guide finds that there is no single model for a national PID strategy, but identifies critical components: a clear value proposition with use cases, a group or organisation responsible for driving the work, an open and inclusive process that involves all stakeholders, and a roadmap with practical steps for implementation. Its checklist covers funding, communication, benchmarking, monitoring and international engagement.

## Knowledge Exchange

Knowledge Exchange, a partnership of six national organisations in Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom, published a study of risk and trust in the PID infrastructure in 2023[@rep-BuildingPlane-23]. It recommends more coordination through national or supranational PID strategies. Its key recommendation to national stakeholders is to establish a national PID roadmap, with a governance body such as a PID advisory board, and to learn from other countries, for example through the RDA. It also proposes an international PID federation with a PID observatory that keeps an up-to-date picture of the PID landscape, and asks PID service providers for transparent business models and plans for continuity if a service ends.
