# Why PID recommendations for Sweden?

_Last updated: 2026-09-30_

A persistent identifier (PID) is a lasting and unique reference to something, such as a publication, a dataset, a researcher or an organisation. Unlike an ordinary web link, a PID is designed to keep working even when things move. PIDs also connect things to each other: a dataset to the article that uses it, a researcher to their work, and a project to its funding.

## Why this matters in Sweden

Sweden has national goals and guidelines for open science. The National Library of Sweden coordinates the national work on open access to research publications. The Swedish Research Council coordinates the national work on open access to research data. PIDs are needed in both areas, and also for things such as software, methods, projects and grants. However, there is a lack of shared recommendations that look at PIDs as a whole: which PID systems to use for different purposes, and how to use them.

## Gaps that shared recommendations can help close

- **Scattered results.** Publications, data and methods are shared through many different channels. Without PIDs, the links between them are easily lost.
- **Uneven use.** The PID systems themselves work well, but organisations and services use them in different ways, often with incomplete information. This makes research harder to find, follow up and give credit for.
- **Manual work.** Information about research often has to be entered or checked by hand, because systems do not share it reliably. PIDs make it possible to enter information once and reuse it.
- **No clear overview.** Many organisations do not have a good overview of all the research outputs and other research-related objects they are responsible for. Consistent PIDs with good descriptions make them easier to keep track of.
- **Duplicated effort.** Without shared solutions, organisations solve the same problems on their own, and those with fewer resources risk falling behind.
- **Decisions made abroad.** Most PID systems are run internationally. Swedish organisations benefit from a shared voice in how these systems develop and are funded.

## What the recommendations aim to do

These recommendations aim to be a shared resource that may be developed collectively over time by actors in the Swedish research sector. The PID landscape is moving along rapidly, and static reports and publications become outdated quickly.

While some national guidance exist for organisations that create PID systems of their own, this resource focuses on the established PID systems that research already depends on. Its [recommendations](../recommendations/index.md) offer research organisations, funders, research infrastructures and national actors a shared starting point. They cover which PID systems to prefer for different purposes, how to build them into everyday systems and routines, and how to coordinate the work nationally and internationally. A section also examines how [other countries](../international/index.md) have approached the same questions.

The aim is research that is easier to find, link, follow up and reuse, with less manual work.
